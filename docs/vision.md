# Project Brief

## Working Title

AI Adoption Blueprint

## Mission

Create a practical website that helps leaders and architects guide people from
safe individual AI use to trusted organizational capability. The site explains
how that journey changes workflows, team practices, strategy, software
architecture, security, delivery, and production operations.

## Audience

Primary readers are AI architects, solution architects, CTOs, engineering and
product leaders, AI practice leaders, consultants, and internal champions
responsible for helping teams adopt AI well. Individual practitioners are an
important entry audience: their practical use of AI is where the journey
starts, but the site helps their leaders and teams turn useful habits into
trusted shared capability.

## Primary Promise

Help serious teams move from scattered experimentation to measurable,
governed, production-grade AI adoption without treating more autonomy as the
default goal.

## Differentiation

This site is not an AI news, prompt-engineering, or generic tool-list site. It
uses concise field notes to explain one decision or practice at a time, then
links to deeper implementation guides when a task genuinely needs technical
depth. Its focus is operating models, system design, production constraints,
security, adoption psychology, and implementation playbooks.

## Adoption Model

The canonical model lives in adoption-stages.md:

- Foundation: Safe Participation.
- Stage 1: Personal Leverage.
- Stage 2: Shared Team Practice.
- Stage 3: Managed Workflows.
- Stage 4: Governed Organizational Capability.

Stages describe the audience's journey. Safety, trust, quality and evaluation,
permissions and context, ownership, observability, ROI, and operating model
are cross-cutting practices introduced at the level each stage needs.

AI authority is assessed separately from adoption maturity:

Advisory → Human-approved → Bounded action → Delegated action.

A workflow should stop at the level justified by its risk and value. An
organization can be mature in one workflow and early in another.

## Non-Goals

- Do not become a generic prompt, extension, skill, or tool directory.
- Do not imply that every workflow should become autonomous.
- Do not use the stages as a compliance score or a claim that an entire company
  has one maturity level.

## Tone

Direct, practical, candid, and grounded. The writing can be personal and opinionated, but should stay useful and avoid overclaiming.

## Non-Goals

- Do not become a generic AI news site.
- Do not create broad claims without having it grounded by evidence or real-world examples.
- Do not optimize for hype-heavy social media framing at the expense of technical credibility.
