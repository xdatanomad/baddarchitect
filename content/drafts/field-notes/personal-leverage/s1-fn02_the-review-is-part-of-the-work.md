# The Review Is Part of the Work.

**SEO title:** The Review Is Part of the Work: Verify AI-Assisted Work  
**Description:** Replace vague human review with a task-specific verification compact for checking AI-assisted factual, analytical, policy, creative, and technical work.  
**Canonical slug:** `/field-notes/review-is-part-of-the-work/`  
**Reader/job:** Individual practitioners and leaders helping them use AI-assisted outputs responsibly when factual accuracy, analytical judgment, policy boundaries, or consequential action matter.  

**Takeaway:** Define what you will check, which source or test you will trust, and when you will stop before you rely on an AI-prepared result.

**Decision rule:** Do not use an AI-prepared result for a consequential decision or action if you cannot state what must be checked, identify the current authoritative source or test, and recognize the condition that requires you to stop or escalate.

An AI-prepared result can look finished long before it is ready to use.

A customer-risk brief confidently repeats a commitment from last month's notes. A cost analysis says spending fell but overlooks a change in transaction volume. A policy summary gives the general rule and misses the exception that applies to this employee. A campaign draft follows the requested tone but includes a product claim that was never approved.

Each result is fluent. Each is plausible. A careful person might read each one twice and still miss the problem.

The failure is not simply that the person did not look hard enough. The practice never defined what a useful review required.

“Review the output” is advice. It is not a verification method.

## Design the Review Before the Result

Human review is often treated as the safety step added after AI has done its work. The person receives a polished answer, gives it a final read, and decides whether it feels right.

That asks too much of intuition.

The right check depends on the work. A factual brief needs traceable, current sources. An analysis needs its inputs, assumptions, and calculations inspected. Policy work needs the authoritative rule and applicable exceptions. Creative work still has constraints: the brief, audience, rights, brand, and claims.

The consequence matters too. A rough list of workshop ideas does not need the same review as a customer escalation, a financial recommendation, a policy interpretation, or a code change.

Before using AI for a recurring task, answer three questions:

- What could be wrong even if the result looks convincing?
- What evidence, calculation, or test would reveal that problem?
- What uncertainty or failure would make me stop rather than keep editing?

Those answers turn review from a vague responsibility into part of the [personal practice](/field-notes/prompt-is-a-moment-personal-practice-is-a-method/).

## The Personal Verification Compact

The **Personal Verification Compact** is a small, private-by-default agreement you make with yourself for one AI-assisted practice. It records what AI may prepare, what you must check, and what must remain your decision.

It is not a legal contract, corporate policy, approval form, or certification that the practice is safe. Completing it does not replace professional judgment. It gives that judgment a repeatable place in the work.

Copy the prompts below into an approved notes system, local Markdown file, or other permitted workspace. Short, specific answers are enough.

### 1. Work and Consequence

- What task may AI help prepare?
- How will I use the result, and who could be affected?
- What is the plausible consequence if the result is wrong, incomplete, stale, or used in the wrong context?

The consequence sets the depth of the review. If you cannot tolerate the likely failure, narrow the use or do the work another way.

### 2. Evidence

- Which sources are allowed for this task?
- Which source is authoritative when information conflicts?
- What must be current, and how will I confirm its freshness?
- Can I trace material claims back to the source that supports them?

An AI response can point you toward evidence. It should not silently become the evidence.

### 3. Checks

- Which claims, calculations, recommendations, or output elements must I verify?
- What specific source check, calculation, test, comparison, or quality bar will I use?
- Which assumptions or counterexamples could change the conclusion?

“Read it again” may be one step. It is rarely the whole check.

### 4. Authority and Stop Conditions

- What data or actions are prohibited?
- What may AI prepare, and what decision or consequential action must remain mine?
- What uncertainty, conflict, missing source, failed test, or unusual case requires me to stop or escalate?
- What must I explicitly confirm before anything is sent, published, approved, or changed?

The compact should make the stopping point easier to recognize while you still have time to use it.

### 5. Learning

- Where will I record a correction, recurring failure, or changed source?
- What should change before the next run?
- When will I review, narrow, pause, or retire this compact?

A compact should change when the work changes. A stale verification method can create the same false confidence as a stale source.

## Different Work Needs Different Checks

The compact stays small because the details belong to the task. These hypothetical examples show why “human-reviewed” does not describe one consistent activity.

| Type of work | Plausible failure | A useful verification pattern |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Factual** | A customer brief carries forward an outdated renewal commitment from old meeting notes. | Trace each material commitment and date to the current approved source. Confirm the reporting period and mark unresolved conflicts rather than smoothing them over. |
| **Analytical** | A cost summary reports a decrease without accounting for lower transaction volume. | Check the input period, definitions, denominator, formulas, and assumptions. Recalculate the decision-driving numbers and test whether another explanation fits the data. |
| **Policy or compliance** | A summary states the general travel rule but misses a regional or role-specific exception. | Open the current authoritative policy, confirm its effective date and scope, check known exceptions, and escalate when the case requires interpretation. |
| **Creative or communication** | A polished campaign draft introduces an unsupported product claim or language too close to protected source material. | Check the approved brief, substantiation for claims, audience, brand constraints, rights, and the meaning a reasonable reader could take from the final wording. |

The pattern is proportional, not ceremonial. You are identifying the failure that matters for this use and choosing a check capable of finding it.

Technical work often combines several patterns. An engineer asking AI to investigate a bug still needs to reproduce the issue, confirm expected behavior, inspect the proposed change, and run the relevant tests. They may also need to review security implications, unintended scope, and whether the test itself proves the requirement.

A passing test is evidence. It is not automatic permission to accept the change.

## Keep the Source of Truth Outside the Answer

Verification gets harder when the relevant context is scattered across old chats, copied notes, and tool-managed memory. A remembered answer may be useful as a lead, but it is weak evidence for consequential work when its source, date, or permission boundary is unclear.

Two lightweight aids can help:

- A **Personal Context Pack** holds the current, permitted references and instructions needed for the practice.
- A **Work Log** records the decisions, actions, corrections, and open state that you deliberately choose to retain.

They serve different purposes. The Context Pack supports the present task. The Work Log preserves selected operational memory for the next one. Neither should become an uncurated dump of every document, nor should either be confused with opaque provider-managed chat memory.

The practical boundary is simple: if AI recalls a material fact but you cannot locate its current authorized source, treat the fact as unverified.

The [Personal Context Pack and Work Log guide](/guides/build-a-personal-context-pack-and-work-log/) covers the setup in more depth. A [Personal AI Playbook](/guides/build-a-personal-ai-playbook/) can then link the compact, context, and reusable method without forcing everything into one long prompt.

## Turn Corrections Into Better Boundaries

Finding an error is not only cleanup. It is information about the practice.

If the customer brief used stale notes, update the source and freshness rule. If the cost analysis hid a denominator, add that calculation to the required checks. If a policy exception was easy to miss, record the escalation condition. If a code change passed the obvious test but expanded scope, add the missing inspection step.

Record the correction in the place that changes the next run:

- update the Verification Compact when the check or stop condition was weak;
- update the Context Pack when a source, permission, or freshness rule changed;
- update the Work Log when a decision, correction, or open question matters later;
- update the Personal Practice Canvas when the task, boundary, or method itself needs to change.

The goal is not to document every interaction. It is to preserve the small amount of learning that makes the next result easier to judge.

## Keep the Stage 1 Boundary Clear

A Personal Verification Compact helps one person earn appropriate confidence in a bounded piece of AI-assisted work. It is not a production evaluation suite, audit trail, shared team control, or substitute for professional and organizational requirements.

If a practice becomes shared, runs without active supervision, touches sensitive systems, or carries material failure cost, personal review is no longer enough. The workflow needs clearer ownership, repeatable evaluation, permission controls, failure handling, and operational accountability.

At Stage 1, the boundary is smaller. AI may collect, analyze, draft, or prepare. You inspect the relevant evidence, make the consequential decision, and confirm the action.

Do not ask only whether the output looks right.

Ask what could make it wrong, which evidence could show you, and when you will stop.

That review is not work added after the AI task.

It is part of the work.
